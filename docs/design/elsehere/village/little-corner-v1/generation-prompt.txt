Use case: stylized-concept. Design a beautiful, distinctive, compact playable TOWN for Elsehere, a 3D language-learning game populated by the tiny-bodied, huge-headed human characters in the attached roster. ONE finished landscape environment concept image. Attached image role: CHARACTER PROPORTION, MATERIAL AND COLOR REFERENCE ONLY. Do not reproduce the roster layout or white character sheet. The town is the main subject. Concept name in very small unobtrusive text at lower left: "elsehere · LITTLE CORNER v1".

ART DIRECTION:
A warm, delightful real-time 3D miniature neighborhood, tactile but clean matte game art, smooth beveled building edges, broad readable surfaces, simple modeled shapes, low-detail wood and plaster, no photo textures, weathering, fine foliage, fuzzy fabric, cinematic realism or busy decorations. The town must look like it was made for these particular adorably blank-faced, gigantic-headed people: broad soft architecture and huge friendly openings above squat lower volumes, tiny practical furniture. Buildings feel pleasantly stout with slightly overhanging SOFT THICK CAPS for roofs, echoing the characters' giant-head/tiny-body mass balance without literal faces or hair on houses. This oversized roof plus compact wall rhythm should repeat consistently across the neighborhood. An original coherent game setting, not a copy of a famous game or movie.

CAMERA AND COMPOSITION:
Landscape 3:2. Elevated three-quarter game-environment view around 35 degrees looking down, low enough that building facades and little character faces are visible, high enough that paths are clear. Frame one whole intimate pedestrian square and its FOUR primary buildings, all approachable and not blocking each other. Not a floating island, not a cross-section dollhouse, not a map UI. The streets and lawns continue softly beyond the edges. Backdrop is a few low rounded hills, soft pale sky, sparse stylized trees. No vast empty field or monumental scenery. Bright mild morning daylight, soft shadows, gentle neutral color grading, clear spatial depth. Buildings and character proportions legible.

SPATIAL PLAN:
An asymmetrical rounded courtyard at center with a SMALL circular tree planter and a low curved bench, plenty of clear open walking space around it. Four short direct pedestrian paths connect to the courtyard, creating a tiny understandable loop. Broad pale cream paving with occasional softly rounded large tiles, low sage grass edges. No roads or cars. Keep the square small enough to see the cafe from the player's front doorstep, supporting a simple directions-giving conversation.

BUILDINGS:
FRONT LEFT — PLAYER HOME: one-story dusty periwinkle-blue cottage, low broad warm-ivory curved roof cap, broad doorway with a softly rounded rectangular top, large low wide window glowing softly, just two small steps or one shallow ramp to the door. A small little planter and porch. Starting point for the player.
BACK LEFT — CAFE: a squat peach/cream cafe, large softly domed terracotta roof cap with shallow overhang, wide arched-ish rectangular doorway, generous rounded window displaying a very simple interior counter. Small solid coffee-cup pictogram sign plus clean readable text "CAFE". Short muted coral awning and TWO tiny outdoor cafe tables with low chairs, kept to one side of the path. The cafe has the most immediately recognizable silhouette for navigation, without a giant novelty cup building.
BACK RIGHT — HOTEL: a compact two-story cream-and-muted-lavender hotel, width greater than height, slightly taller than other buildings but still squat, deep dusky-blue rounded roof cap. Three broad upper windows, small plain railing, one large central rounded entry and clear readable "HOTEL" sign with a small key pictogram. Entry under a short broad canopy, glimpse of a low reception desk inside. No palace, clock tower or extravagant architectural detailing.
FRONT RIGHT — GENERAL STORE: a small warm ochre and cream shop with thick pale sage roof cap, a wide open front window, one broad entrance, simple "SHOP" sign and small tote-bag pictogram. A very small group of 3 readable produce boxes under the window, not a crowded market.

UNIFIED MODULAR GEOMETRY:
Use the same family of broad rounded-rectangle doors and windows on all four buildings, same thick wall edges, same 2-3 repeating window sizes, repeatable porch/awning frames. Differentiation through roof silhouette, color block and signs rather than intricate bespoke structure. Doors must be wide enough for enormous heads: clear opening at least 1.3 times a character's head width and 1.4 times their TOTAL body height. Buildings about 2.5-4 character heights tall. No narrow realistic human-sized door slots with heads too large to pass. No tiny high windows or super-tall shop counters. Seats and counters low for the little bodies; two characters can comfortably pass in every main path. Trees have a few smooth chunky leaf clusters on short stout trunks, no leaves rendered individually. Reuse one small planter style and one low bench style.

CHARACTERS:
Include SIX small residents from the attached roster at believable scale: the periwinkle-jacket female player near her blue home; the matching short-haired male player and coral-jacket traveler facing one another on the central open path, suggesting a directions conversation; dark-skinned curly-haired terracotta-top barista standing next to cafe entrance; navy-uniform tan-skinned hotelier beside hotel entry; auburn-bob mustard-cardigan shopkeeper beside shop. Characters must retain huge heads roughly 70-75% of overall height, tiny bodies and limbs, small widely spaced OR clustered graphic eyes, tiny slender noses, little curved smiles. Do not turn them into generic anime people with large eyes or realistic long adult bodies. They are residents of this world, not foreground giant cutout portraits. Keep 1-2 central faces readable. No speech bubbles or UI.

PALETTE:
Warm ivory plaster and cream paths as the main base. Periwinkle, muted coral, sage, ochre and dark cocoa in restrained medium-saturation blocks, directly harmonizing with the clothing in the reference. Avoid sugary pink overload, neon colors, all-beige monotony or dark medieval fantasy. The mood is approachable, a little quirky, calm and welcoming to people practicing a new language. Crisp appealing 3D game art with plenty of uncluttered space for movement and conversation.

DELIVERABLE:
ONE cohesive finished town overview, not a collage, not separate building cards, not a blueprint. Show the cafe, hotel, shop, home, central meeting space and their walkable connection together. Minimal text: building signs and small bottom concept label only.
